# Company Profile: Apex Logistics Solutions (ALS)
## Apex Logistics Solutions is a third-party logistics (3PL) brokerage firm that coordinates freight transportation, tracks shipments, and manages supply chain data for regional manufacturers.
•	Corporate Structure: Single location (Headquarters)
•	Total Employees: 50
•	Operating Hours: Monday – Friday, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (Local Time)
•	Work Model: 100% In-office (No permanent remote workers, though secure emergency access is required)

The 50 employees are divided into four distinct functional groups, each requiring different technology configurations:
•	Logistics Brokers (35 employees): The core revenue drivers. They require high-bandwidth, low-latency connections to run real-time freight-matching software, dual monitors for multitasking, and softphone (VoIP) capabilities to make hundreds of calls daily.
•	Finance & HR (7 employees): Handle payroll, billing, and sensitive employee data. They require high security, access to accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks Online), and dedicated local printing/scanning access.
•	Account Managers & Operations (6 employees): Manage high-value client relationships and resolve shipping disputes. They rely heavily on email, CRM software, and collaborative video conferencing.
•	Executive Leadership (2 employees): Require mobile flexibility (laptops/tablets) and high-level data dashboards to monitor company performance.

Technical Requirements & Constraints
Because the business operates strictly on a weekdays, 8-to-5 schedule, your study should account for the following network and infrastructure demands:
1. Network Traffic Profile
•	Peak Demand: Demand spikes sharply at 7:45 AM as employees log in and drops off drastically by 5:15 PM.
•	Off-Hours Window: 5:01 PM to 7:59 AM weekdays, and the entire weekend, are ideal for automated cloud backups, software patching, and system updates to prevent daytime slowdowns.
2. Core Infrastructure Needs
•	Internet Connectivity: A primary Dedicated Internet Access (DIA) fiber line (e.g., 300 Mbps symmetrical) to handle heavy VoIP traffic and cloud applications, paired with a lower-cost business cable broadband line for automatic failover.
•	Networking Hardware: A business-class firewall/router, a 48-port PoE+ (Power over Ethernet) switch to power VoIP phones and desktop lines, and 3–4 centrally managed Wi-Fi Access Points for internal/guest use.
•	Software Stack (SaaS-Heavy): To minimize on-premise server maintenance, the company utilizes cloud productivity suites (Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace), a cloud-based CRM (like Salesforce or HubSpot), and a cloud VoIP system (like RingCentral or Zoom Phone).
